# Leftovers Chili

|               |   |
|---------------|---|
| Rating        | 4 |
| Ease          | 4 |
| Quickness     | 2 |
| Affordability | 4 |

An "AFK-friendly" clean-the-fridge crockpot chili. Usually built from taco leftovers, but any similar cooked meat and vegetable leftovers work. Dried beans stretch it a long way, and the tomatillos and peppers keep it bright instead of just tomato-y.

# Ingredients

|Ingredient|Amount|Notes|
|-|-|-|
|Leftover Meat and Vegetables|—|Usually taco leftovers; any similar cooked meat/veg|
|Mixed Dried Beans|—|Cooked until tender before they go in the pot|
|Crushed Tomatoes|1 large can||
|Diced Tomatoes|1 large can||
|Tomatillos|4–6 large|Husked and cut up|
|Mild/Medium Peppers|Similar count to the tomatillos|Poblano, jalapeno, etc. — for texture|
|Spicy Peppers or Hot Sauce|—|To taste, for heat|
|Worcestershire Sauce|—|For depth; the heat comes from the peppers and hot sauce|
|Garlic, Onion, Cumin, etc.|—|Whatever chili spices are out|
|Whatever Else Strikes Your Fancy|—|Beans, corn, squash, extra leftovers — go ahead|
|Salt and Pepper|||

# Directions

- Cook the dried beans until rehydrated and tender; drain.
- Cut up the fresh veg — tomatillos, mild peppers, any aromatics.
- Dump the beans, tomatoes, tomatillos, peppers, leftovers, spices, and Worcestershire sauce in a crockpot.
- Stir occasionally until hungry — low and slow works best.
- Adjust the heat with spicy peppers or hot sauce, season with salt and pepper, and serve.

# Other Considerations

If the chili ends up too thin, leave the lid off for the last while or mash some of the beans against the side of the pot. Too thick — add a splash of water or the juice from the tomato cans. Tomatillos bring acidity; taste near the end and balance with a bit of sugar if they come on too strong. Freezes well, which is the whole point of making chili from leftovers.
